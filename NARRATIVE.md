# Liminal Engine — the story

This is the document everything else has to answer to. If a visual, a sound or a line of text cannot be traced back to something here, it goes.

---

## The idea in one paragraph

The alchemists called quicksilver *Mercurius*: the mind. Fluid, bright, restless, impossible to hold. The whole of the Great Work, in their own words, was to **fix the volatile**: to still the quicksilver until it turned to gold and became a mirror. They wrote it as chemistry. It was always about attention. Liminal Engine puts a sphere of quicksilver in front of you and gives you one power over it: your stillness. Move, and it shatters. Stop, and it gathers, settles, warms, and finally turns to gold and shows you your own face. You can only see yourself when you stop reaching for the mirror. That is the piece.

## Why it matters now

Every screen in your life is built to keep the quicksilver moving. This one is built to let it stop. It takes the same parts, a camera, a screen, a loop that measures you, and inverts the reward: it gives you the most when you give it nothing. Narcissus drowned reaching for his reflection. Here the reflection arrives only when the reaching ends.

## The three layers

Every moment in the piece works on three layers at once. If one is missing the moment is decoration.

| Layer | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **What you see and hear** | The sphere, the rings, the drum, the tone, the words |
| **What it means** | The alchemical stage, in the alchemists' own language |
| **What it is doing to you** | The attention effect: what your body and mind are being led to do |

## The arc

Ninety seconds. Five movements. The visitor's own stillness drives the progress; the text and drum drive the story. Nothing is on rails: a visitor who fidgets stays in the dark, and the piece tells them so.

### 0. The threshold (before touch)

*See*: the sphere at rest, black quicksilver, slowly turning, in a dark room. The rings carry the sentences of previous visitors, drifting past. A title and one line: *A sphere of quicksilver that turns to gold when you are still.* A button: *Touch to begin.*
*Means*: the prima materia. Nothing has happened yet.
*Does*: curiosity. Strangers' sentences are the hook (*"I heard my own heartbeat"*). You want to know what they saw.

### 1. The lesson (0 to 10 seconds)

The first ten seconds teach the only rule, by making you do it. This is the hook for someone who has never sat still in front of a screen in their life.

*See*: the drum starts at four beats a second, the shamanic tempo. One word: **Move.** You move. The sphere **shatters** into a thousand droplets on the beat. Then: **Now stop.** You stop. The droplets fall back and gather. Then: *This is quicksilver. The alchemists called it the mind. Tonight you are going to fix it.*
*Means*: *solve et coagula*, the whole method, shown once.
*Does*: agency. Your body has learned the piece responds to it instantly. You are already inside the loop.

### 2. Caput Corvi, the raven (10 to 30 seconds)

*See*: black, boiling tar. The drum is fast and the rings hit with it. Colour: none, except the ember inside. Text, one line at a time, timed to the drum: *Everything you carried in here is in the sphere.* / *The message you did not answer. The thing you said.* / *Let it burn. Do not move.* If you move: it shatters, the text says *Again.* and the stage does not advance.
*Means*: the Raven's Head, the alchemists' emblem for the first stage: the black bird that sits on the sealed vessel before anything can begin. The work starts in the dark, with what you brought.
*Does*: naming what is in the mind lets it be set down. This is the oldest trick in every contemplative tradition, and the reason a visitor will feel the piece is about them.

### 3. Albedo, the whitening: dissolution (30 to 50 seconds)

*See*: the sphere loses its edge. It **dissolves** into orbiting silver droplets and the rings begin to **multiply**: three become six, twelve, a slow kaleidoscope, a mandala opening around the centre. The chosen tone comes up under the drum; the drum slows toward a walking pace. Text: *Dissolve.* / *You do not have to hold it together.* / *Follow the drum.*
*Means*: *solve*. The washing. The one thing becomes many.
*Does*: the psychedelic phase. Symmetry, slow rotation and a steady pulse are what the eye locks onto; boundaries soften. This is the "experience" and it is earned by stillness, not handed out.

### 4. Citrinitas, the yellowing: the turn inward (50 to 70 seconds)

*See*: the mandala pulls inward, a slow spiral toward the centre. Warmth comes into the silver. The drum slows to a resting heartbeat. Text: *You are the only thing in this room that is still.* / *Everything else is moving around you.* / *Go inward.* On the inner ring, the letters V.I.T.R.I.O.L. turn to face you: *Visita Interiora Terrae Rectificando Invenies Occultum Lapidem*. Visit the interior of the earth; by setting things right you will find the hidden stone.
*Means*: the dawn. The alchemists' instruction to look inward for the stone.
*Does*: the spiral and the slowing beat pull attention to a single point. Breath follows the drum without being told.

### 5. Rubedo, the reddening: coagulation (70 to 90 seconds)

*See*: the drum **stops**. Silence, then the tone alone. The droplets **coagulate**, many become one, the mandala folds back into three rings, and the sphere turns gold and goes perfectly still. It is a mirror. In it: you, and the room behind you. Text: *Solve et coagula.* / *This is what is left when you stop.* Then nothing, for ten full seconds. Just you in the gold.
*Means*: the stone. *Fixing the volatile*. Gold is not the reward; the mirror is.
*Does*: the silence after ninety seconds of rhythm is the loudest moment in the piece. The visitor sees their own face inside the thing they have been staring at. Most people will hold their breath.

### 6. The question

*What did you notice in the space between thoughts?* One sentence. It is engraved on the outer ring for the next person, in their own words, in italic. Over a show the rings fill with strangers' noticings. The stone is not the gold; it is what people found.

## Why each element exists

- **The drum (bellows)**: the spine. Four beats a second at the start, the tempo of shamanic drumming across cultures; slowing to a resting heart; silent at gold. Everything visual pulses on it. It is the reason the body follows the piece without instruction.
- **The tone (harmony of the spheres)**: the visitor chooses one of six, each a planet and its metal. It is the *colour* of their sitting, in sound and in light. The choice is small but it is theirs, and the piece says so: *You chose Venus. Copper. The green of the sea.*
- **Shatter and gather**: the mechanic *is* the metaphor. Solve et coagula, every time you move and stop.
- **The kaleidoscope**: dissolution made visible. One becomes many (albedo), then many become one (rubedo). It is not decoration; it is the middle of the story.
- **The mirror**: the payoff and the argument. You see yourself only when you stop.
- **The text voice**: short, second person, present tense, timed to the drum. It never explains. It tells you what is happening to you and what to do. No neuroscience, no wellness. The alchemists' words where they exist.
- **The inscriptions**: the collective stone. Proof to the next visitor that something happens here.
- **The colour of the room**: the chosen tone tints the light on the object and the halo behind it, not a flat backdrop, so the piece still works on a **holographic fan** (pure black behind the object) and on a screen or projector.

## The hypnotic craft

The arc is an induction, built the way inductions are built. Every stage has to do its job or the trance breaks.

- **Fixation**: one object, centred, always. Nothing else on screen asks for the eye.
- **Yes-set**: *Move.* / *Now stop.* Two instructions the visitor cannot fail. Compliance is established in eight seconds and the voice has authority from then on.
- **Pacing, then leading**: the drum starts fast, matching the state a person walks in with. Only once the body has locked to it does it begin to slow. Breath follows tempo; tempo is ours.
- **Fractionation**: every shatter and gather is a small exit and re-entry. Each return goes a little deeper, and the piece uses the visitor's own fidgeting to do it.
- **Deepening**: the spiral inward, the mandala, the slowing beat, the voice getting quieter and slower. Fewer words per line as the sitting goes on.
- **The suggestion**: second person, present tense, always. *You are the only thing in this room that is still.* Not "try to be". Statements of fact the body then makes true.
- **The convincer**: the mirror. Undeniable evidence that something happened, arriving at the point of deepest stillness.
- **The post-hypnotic suggestion**: the question. *What did you notice?* presupposes that they noticed something. They will find it.

The rhythm is the induction; the story is what it carries. If the drum is not felt in the body the rest is just pictures.

## What is not in the piece

Menus beyond the tone. Ratings. Statistics. Claims about the brain. More than one font for the visitor. Anything that asks for attention while the work is happening.

## The line on the wall

> The alchemists called quicksilver the mind: bright, fluid, impossible to hold. Their whole art was to still it until it turned to gold and became a mirror. Move, and this sphere shatters. Be still, and it will show you your face.
